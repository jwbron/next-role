# Compensation and negotiation preparation

Prepare around the candidate's priorities and real alternatives. Published ranges and salary reports inform questions, they aren't an offer or a promised outcome.

## Before an offer

Ask what matters: recurring cash, location, scope, level, autonomy, stability, hours, benefits, learning, or equity upside. A candidate can decline to record a target or floor. Keep any private threshold out of employer-facing drafts.

Prepare questions about the role's approved range, level, compensation components, and process. Ask the candidate whether they want to state a range, seek the employer's range first, or defer until scope is clearer. Don't supply a number on their behalf. Salary-history rules differ by jurisdiction, don't assume a disclosure is required or lawful to request.

## Establish actual terms

Use written terms where available and record the source/date. Distinguish verbal indications, target compensation, a formal offer, and conditions. Clarify deadline timezone, start date, level, base currency and period, bonus guarantees, signing terms, equity instrument, vesting, benefits, remote policy, on-call, and travel.

Equity questions include issuer, instrument, units, strike price for options, vesting/cliff, exercise window, tax treatment, refresh policy, dilution, sale eligibility, and restrictions. A financing valuation, acquisition, or tender headline doesn't establish employee liquidity.

## Compare like with like

Keep first-year and recurring cash separate. Normalize currency only with an explicit rate and date, don't quietly mix currencies. A target bonus isn't guaranteed. If the guarantee is part of the target bonus, don't add it twice.

Synthetic example: 120,000 annual base, 10,000 guaranteed bonus included in a 15,000 target bonus, and 8,000 first-year signing gives 138,000 first-year guaranteed cash and 143,000 first-year target cash. Recurring guaranteed cash is 130,000 only if the guarantee continues, and recurring target cash is 135,000. A four-year 80,000 grant vesting evenly is 20,000 nominal annual equity, not 80,000 per year or guaranteed proceeds.

Model actual vesting by year where it isn't even. Treat private equity as scenarios, not cash. Identify excluded taxes, exercise costs, benefit values, and risk. A gross comparison isn't take-home pay.

## Make an ask

Clarify missing material terms before countering. Prioritize a few requests and their reasons, then identify acceptable alternatives. Consider level, scope, start date, signing payment, review timing, time off, remote terms, and other non-pay needs alongside base salary.

Draft a concise expression of interest and a concrete request. Use genuine competing options and actual deadlines only with permission. Never invent an offer, bluff a deadline, or imply willingness to walk away that the candidate hasn't confirmed. Don't send the draft.

## Decide

Compare the work and package against the candidate's own criteria. Show what could change the recommendation. Request final written terms after an agreed change and check contingencies before treating it as settled. Flag legal, immigration, and tax questions for qualified advice. The candidate makes acceptance, rejection, resignation, and disclosure decisions.
