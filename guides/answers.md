# Tailor without inventing

Start from the exact prompt, current candidate draft, and confirmed evidence. A job description is evidence of the employer's needs, not of the candidate's experience.

## Resume selection

Map the role's most important work to specific responsibilities, decisions, and results. Change the selection, order, and level of detail before changing keywords. Use the employer's terminology only when it accurately describes the work. Preserve actual titles and dates. Career changes can use projects, education, volunteer work, research, or transferable responsibilities with honest attribution.

A useful accomplishment shows the problem, the candidate's action or decision, and an outcome. An observed qualitative result is better than an invented number. For metrics, retain period, baseline, units, and limitations. Distinguish a team's result from individual ownership and a prototype from a deployed system.

Don't promise an applicant tracking system (ATS) score or screening outcome. Use readable text, ordinary headings, and truthful terminology rather than hidden keywords.

## Motivation and letters

Ask what genuinely interests the candidate. Connect that interest to the actual work and one relevant example. A letter should add a reason or context that the resume doesn't already provide. Don't write one merely because a template exists.

Don't diagnose a company's private problems from marketing copy. Don't infer personal beliefs from technical projects. A candidate can be curious about a role without claiming lifelong passion.

## Behavioral answers

Use a specific event. Establish the situation, the candidate's responsibility, the decision or action, the outcome, and what they learned if they confirm it. Keep technical detail proportional to the question. Avoid claiming another person's implementation, a managerial title the candidate didn't hold, or a lesson they never stated.

## Forms and restrictions

Record the exact question, required flag if known, limits, source, date, and AI policy. If the form can't be read, request pasted questions or a redacted screenshot. Leave legal, demographic, consent, sponsorship, and other sensitive fields for the candidate unless they explicitly provide an answer and request help.

If original candidate drafting is required, stop at permitted clarification and request their draft. Later manual edits don't retroactively authorize a prohibited initial AI answer.

## Final pass

Read the answer aloud, compare it with the candidate's voice, remove repeated evidence across answers, and check every claim. Keep private doubts, negotiation targets, other interview processes, and submission notes out of upload text. Rebuild changed PDFs and reconcile mirrored answers before handoff.
